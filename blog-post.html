ACH, RTP or SWIFT? How to pick the right rail for every payout

Speed, cost and reach pull in different directions. Here's a practical way to route each payment, and what to do when a rail lets you down.

MCMaya ChenHead of Payments
Guide›Sep 22, 20263 min read
On this page
On this page ▾

Every payout travels on a rail: the network that actually moves money from your account to someone else's. Most teams pick one rail when they set up payments and never look at it again. That's usually where the hidden cost and the late payments come from.

The right rail depends on three things: where the payee's bank is, how fast they need the money, and how much a delay or a failure would cost you. Get those three answers and the choice mostly makes itself.

01Why rail choice matters

Two payments of the same amount to the same country can differ by days in arrival time and by several percent in total cost, depending only on how they're sent. At scale, that difference becomes real money and real frustration for payees.

  • Speed decides whether a contractor gets paid on Friday or the following Wednesday.
  • Cost includes the transfer fee, and for cross-border payments, the FX spread and any intermediary bank fees.
  • Reliability is about how often payments bounce and how clearly the rail tells you why.

02The main rails at a glance

These are typical characteristics. Exact cut-off times and limits vary by bank and provider.

03Speed versus cost

Faster isn't always better. For a monthly supplier run with a known due date, a slower and cheaper rail is fine as long as you send early enough. For a driver who finished a shift today, a few days' wait costs you trust.

“Rail choice is a per-payment decision. Treat it like a per-account setting and you'll either overpay or underdeliver.”

— Maya Chen, Head of Payments

04When SWIFT still wins

Local rails are cheaper and faster, but they only work where you, or your provider, can reach the local banking system. For the long tail of countries, or for very large transfers that need a full audit trail through correspondent banks, SWIFT is still the right tool.

The trick is to use it on purpose. Show the payee the expected arrival window up front, and choose whether you or they cover intermediary fees so the amount that lands isn't a surprise.

05Plan for failures

Every rail rejects payments sometimes: a closed account, a name mismatch, a network outage. What matters is how quickly you find out and whether the retry is safe. Always send payouts with an idempotency_key so a retry can never pay someone twice.

# Retry safely on a backup rail
curl https://api.ledgerly.example/v1/payouts \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: run-0922-pye_82x" \
  -d payee=pye_82x -d amount=125000 -d currency=EUR \
  -d rail_preference=sepa_instant,swift
Key takeaway

Send with a rail preference list, not a single rail. If the first choice is down, the payment moves to the next one automatically, and your idempotency key keeps it from being sent twice.

06A simple decision rule

  1. If the payee's country has a real-time rail and the payment is time-sensitive, use it.
  2. If it's a scheduled run with a known due date, use the cheapest local rail and send early.
  3. If there's no local rail, use SWIFT and tell the payee when to expect the money.
  4. Always list a fallback, and always send with an idempotency key.
